# BL-11 — Near-simultaneous multi-peer pairing stalls, though sequenced pairing works

## Context

Found 2026-07-25 during a full real-browser walkthrough of the M2 social scene, the session
immediately after BL-10 closed. BL-10 fixed the single-phone pairing race (claim sent before any
WebRTC peer exists) and was verified with three phones pairing in quick succession — each
submitted a few seconds apart, each claimed within ~6 seconds.

This session then paired three phones by firing all three `pair.claim` submissions in the same
instant (one batched script, no gap). Two of three phones stalled for over 60 seconds:

- The third (first-submitted) phone claimed normally.
- The other two generated **hundreds of `decryptMessage` `OperationError` exceptions** (35 in the
  first burst, growing past 500 by the time the walkthrough moved on) and never received a
  `state.snapshot`.
- Resetting those two phones and re-submitting them a few seconds apart (not simultaneously)
  fixed it immediately — each claimed within 6 seconds, identical to the earlier verification.

## What's understood, and what isn't

Understood: `pair.claim` is sent with no target (the phone can't know which connected peer is the
host until one answers), so it broadcasts to every currently-connected peer via
`createEnvelopeChannel`. In a mesh of one host and three phones, that means claim broadcasts and
their retries (BL-10's every-1s resend) reach other *phones* too, not just the host — each
receiving phone's `PairingClient.receive` tries to decrypt with its own key and throws. This is
expected noise, not itself the bug (see the do_not item below on hardening it anyway).

Not understood: why phone→host WebRTC connections specifically failed to form within 60+ seconds
for two peers when three (or more) join `joinRoom` at nearly the same instant, when the same
connections form in ~6 seconds when joins are staggered by a few seconds. This wasn't
root-caused — it weighs like a trystero/nostr signaling contention or ICE-negotiation collision
under simultaneous multi-peer join, but that's not verified, only the symptom and the staggering
workaround are.

## Frontier resolution (2026-07-26)

Reclassified as frontier work because the fix changes WebRTC room topology. Trystero's upstream
tracker independently records the same failure class: rooms with three or more members can form
partial meshes or miss signaling messages during concurrent joins. Telemetry Engine requires a
phone-to-host star, not a phone-to-phone mesh, so each seat will derive a separate signaling room
from the existing protocol session id and player id. The encrypted protocol session id and pairing
material remain unchanged.

## Do not

- Assume this is BL-10 reopened — BL-10's fix (buffer + retry-until-answered) is confirmed correct
  and is why the *staggered* case now works at all; this is a distinct contention question under
  concurrent joins.
- Silently swallow `PairingClient.receive`'s decrypt exceptions as a fix for this card — that's a
  legitimate separate hardening (an unhandled promise rejection from routine cross-talk is noisy
  and worth a `.catch`) but does not address why the WebRTC connection itself stalls.
- Touch `Visibility`, schemas, or the phase engine.

## Done when

- Root cause identified for why near-simultaneous multi-peer `joinRoom` calls delay or fail
  connection formation compared to staggered joins, OR a deliberate design response is chosen
  (e.g., the shared screen staggers revealing pairing cards, or the phone jitters its first claim)
  with the reasoning recorded.
- The real M2 demo walkthrough (`docs/demos/M2.md`) can pair three phones however players actually
  approach it in a live room — including the case where two people scan/enter codes at
  approximately the same time — without a multi-minute stall.
- `PairingClient.receive`'s decrypt failure gets a `.catch` (or equivalent) so routine cross-talk
  produces no unhandled rejection, independent of whether it turns out to explain the stall.

## Routing

Frontier work (WebRTC transport, per CLAUDE.md's always-frontier list).
